BEFORE THE DEMO (do this once, 5 minutes)
-----------------------------------------
1. Chrome -> chrome://extensions -> turn on "Developer mode" -> "Load unpacked" ->
   choose the lexguard_001-main\extension folder. (Already loaded? Click the reload button.)
   Check the version says 0.5.1.
2. On the LEXGUARD card click "Details" and turn ON "Allow access to file URLs".
   (Needed only for 5_StreamNest_Signup_Page.html, because it opens from your computer.)
3. Click the puzzle-piece icon in Chrome's toolbar and PIN LEXGUARD, so its blue shield is visible.
4. Open these tabs, left to right:
     Tab 1: 5_StreamNest_Signup_Page.html (drag it into Chrome)
     Tab 2: https://razorpay.com/terms/
     Tab 3: https://www.spotify.com/in-en/legal/end-user-agreement/   (backup real site)
5. Do one practice run on Tab 1 and click "Allow" when Chrome asks for page access.
   After that it will not ask again during the demo.
6. Keep the slides open in another window as a backup.
   LEXGUARD works offline, so Wi-Fi is only needed for the real websites in Tabs 2 and 3.


THE DEMO (about 4 minutes)
--------------------------

STEP 1 - The problem on a real-looking signup page  (Tab 1, ~60 s)
  Do:   Click the LEXGUARD shield -> the side panel opens -> click "Check this page".
  See:  Risky sentences turn red (serious) and yellow (medium) on the page itself.
        Score ring: 76/100 HIGH RISK.
        "These terms have 4 serious kinds of risk and 3 more worth checking."
  Say:  "Before I click 'I agree', LEXGUARD reads the terms for me. Red means serious, yellow
         means worth checking. The sentence about my two screens is not highlighted, because
         it's harmless."
  Do:   Click the "Non-refundable payment" card in the panel -> the page jumps to that
        sentence and flashes it. Click a highlight on the page -> the panel jumps to its card.

STEP 2 - Why this score?  (same tab, ~30 s)
  Do:   Click "How was this score worked out?" under the verdict.
  See:  A table: each kind of risk, its level and its points (HIGH +15, MEDIUM +5, LOW +1).
  Say:  "The score is explainable. Each kind of risk counts once, so long documents aren't
         punished for being long."

STEP 3 - A real website  (Tab 2, Razorpay terms, ~60 s)
  Do:   Click "Check this page".
  See:  About 59/100 HIGH RISK (the live page can differ slightly from our tests).
        "What is this?" says these are the rules you agree to on razorpay.com, between you and
        Razorpay Payments Private Limited, about 30,000 words, about 2.5 hours to read.
        Topics in simple words, each marked with its risks or "looks OK".
  Say:  "This is a real 30,000-word document, almost two and a half hours of reading.
         LEXGUARD checks it in about a tenth of a second, inside the browser. Nothing is uploaded."
  Do:   Scroll to "How LEXGUARD checked this" -> open "Not a risk for you".
  Say:  "Our Defender checks WHO a sentence affects. 'You may opt for extra services' is your
         choice, not a risk, so it isn't counted. Only risks that fall on the user are shown."

STEP 4 - An offer letter PDF, with Indian rules  (~60 s)
  Do:   In the side panel click "Paste or upload a document (.txt / .pdf)" -> a checker tab opens
        -> "Upload .txt / .pdf" -> choose 1_Risky_Offer_Letter.pdf.
  See:  81/100 HIGH. Cards tagged "India rule": the service bond, original certificates kept,
        salary withheld, the non-compete.
  Do:   Open "See details" on the bond card.
  Say:  "It quotes the exact sentence, explains it in plain words, gives a question to ask HR,
         and suggests fairer wording, here a pro-rata bond. Indian courts usually allow only
         reasonable compensation, and LEXGUARD tells you that."
  Do:   Click "Copy clarification email" and paste it into Notepad: a ready email to HR.

STEP 5 - Proof it doesn't just shout at everything  (~30 s)
  Do:   Upload 2_Fair_Offer_Letter.pdf.
  See:  2/100 LOW RISK.
  Say:  "Same kind of letter, written fairly: pay changes need my consent, notice is the same
         for both sides, certificates are returned. The Defender finds these protections, so the
         score drops to 2. It isn't a keyword alarm."

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
- "LEXGUARD can't read this tab": refresh the page and click "Check this page" again.
  On the local signup page, check that "Allow access to file URLs" is ON.
- "LEXGUARD needs your permission": click "Check this page" again and choose "Allow".
- No internet: skip Step 3 and use Steps 1, 4 and 5. They work fully offline.
- Chrome's built-in PDF viewer can't be read by extensions. Always upload PDFs in the checker tab.
- Anything else: show slides 4 and 5 (screenshots) and continue.


ONE-LINE ANSWERS FOR JUDGES
---------------------------
Is it AI?            Four agents (Extractor, Risk Finder, Defender, Judge) working on 32 rules,
                     so it's fast, explainable and private. An optional AI layer is next.
Accuracy?            On public data it catches 74% of unfair website terms (UNFAIR-ToS) and 60%
                     of business-contract risks (CUAD). Only 3% of fair sentences are wrongly marked HIGH.
Privacy?             Runs inside the browser. No server, no upload, no AI service.
Why not ChatGPT?     Chatbots can invent things and need your document uploaded. LEXGUARD always
                     quotes the exact sentence and checks protections before warning.
Is it legal advice?  No, it's a review aid that tells you what to ask before signing.
